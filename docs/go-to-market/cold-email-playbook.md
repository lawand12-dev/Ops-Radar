## 1. The Playbook Philosophy

Cold email in 2026 is not dead — generic cold email is. The inbox now filters at four layers: the spam filter, the preview pane, the 1.7-second scan, and the reply decision. An email must survive all four to earn a response.

What works in 2026:

**Personalization beats volume.** Every benchmark we trust says the same thing: a sender doing 30 personalized emails per day outperforms a sender doing 300 templated ones. We've watched it ourselves — the 250+ emails we've sent got 3–5% reply rates because they were researched, not because they were many. The next 250 should be researched harder, not sent faster.

**Dollar + timeline hooks beat problem hooks.** Section 10.1 of the strategy doc is unambiguous: problem hooks ("do you struggle with X?") return 4.39% replies. Timeline + dollar hooks ("by Q3 you're leaving $18K on the table") return 10.01%. That is a 2.3x lift from one rewrite. Agency owners have heard "we help you scale" a thousand times. They have never heard "you're leaking $4K/month on the Acme account and you'll find out in October."

**The AI COO category lets us sidestep generic SaaS pitches.** Every other cold email in an agency owner's inbox is selling them another dashboard, another tool, another "platform that helps you scale." We are selling the role they can't afford to hire — a Chief AI Officer at $400K, a COO at $25K, a fractional COO at $10K, all replaced by software at $500–3,000. That framing makes the email feel like a different category of conversation, not the 47th SaaS pitch this week.

**Why peer-to-peer voice wins.** Agency owners are operators. They smell vendor language at 100 yards. The emails that get replies sound like they came from another founder who happens to have built the thing they need — not a BDR running a sequence. Founder-sent email from an obviously founder-named domain (lawand@opsradar.co) is one of the biggest unfair advantages we have. We should not give it up by sounding like a sequenced robot.

**One ask. One angle. One number per email.** Cold email is not the place to explain everything OpsRadar does. It is the place to make the reader curious enough to click reply. The whole sale happens on the demo. The email's job is to earn the 20 minutes.

**The lift we're chasing:** 4% → 10%+ reply rate. At our current send volume, that is the difference between 10 customers and 25 customers by Day 90 — without sending a single extra email.

## 2. The 5-Touch Sequence

A 14-day, 5-touch sequence: Email 1 → LinkedIn touch → Email 2 → LinkedIn DM → Email 3 (breakup). All emails plain text, no HTML, no images, no signature image. Each one is between 60 and 90 words.

---

### Email 1 — Day 0: Cold intro (timeline + dollar hook)

**Subject:** quick question on margin
**Preview:** noticed something about [agency]

```
[FirstName] — saw [specific signal: e.g., "you just brought on a new PPC lead", "your latest case study on the Acme rebrand", "you're hiring a head of ops"].

That stage usually means one thing: revenue is climbing but per-client margin is getting murky. The agencies we work with were leaking 15–23% of profit on overserviced accounts and not finding out until quarter-end — by then it's $8K–$20K gone.

We built the always-on margin and scope watcher for agencies your size. Plugs into your existing stack.

Worth a 20-min walkthrough next week?

— Lawand, OpsRadar
```

**Why it works:**
- Personalization slot in line 1 is the gate — without a real signal, do not send
- The hook is timeline ("quarter-end") + dollar ($8K–$20K), not "do you struggle with margin?"
- 4 short paragraphs, scannable in 7 seconds on mobile
- CTA is low-friction ("worth a walkthrough?") — a yes/no question is easier than booking a slot
- No company tagline, no link to website, no PDF attachment — earn those later

---

### LinkedIn Touch — Day 2: Connection request + DM

**Connection request copy (300 char limit):**
```
[FirstName] — sent you a note on email about the margin side of [agency]. Following up here too in case email isn't your channel. No pitch.
```

**Follow-up DM if they accept (send 24h after connect):**
```
Thanks for the connect. Quick context — we built an AI COO for agencies your size. Watches client margin, scope, utilization across your stack in real time. Catches the unprofitable accounts in week 2 instead of month 3.

Not asking for a call yet — just curious if this is a problem you're solving today, or one you're planning to.
```

**Why it works:**
- Multi-channel lifts response +287% (Section 10.1)
- The connection request acknowledges the email — doesn't pretend it's a fresh first touch
- "No pitch" disarms the default LinkedIn-spam defense
- The follow-up DM asks a qualifying question, not for a meeting — they can reply in 6 seconds

---

### Email 2 — Day 4: Proof + specific number (different angle)

**Subject:** the 30-day detection lag
**Preview:** 21.5% of agencies lost money last year

```
[FirstName] — different angle on the note from Tuesday.

The number that surprised most agency owners we showed it to: 21.5% of agencies were losing money in 2026, up from 13% the year before. The pattern is the same every time — overservicing on 2–3 accounts, found out 30–90 days too late.

The agencies that turned this around all did the same thing: real-time per-client profitability, not quarterly reviews.

That's what we do.

Open to a 20-min demo Thursday or Friday?

— Lawand
```

**Why it works:**
- Acknowledges the prior email ("different angle") without restating it
- Opens with a hard number from a credible source — Planable 2026 is data the reader can google
- Names the mechanism (30–90 day detection lag) — this is the language inside the strategy doc that defines the problem
- Harder CTA than email 1 — two specific days, easy yes/no
- Still no link, still founder-signed

---

### LinkedIn DM — Day 7: Video walkthrough offer

```
[FirstName] — last poke from me this week. Faster than a call: I can record a 4-min Loom showing what OpsRadar would catch on [agency] specifically, based on what's public (stack guess, team size, services). You watch it on your own time.

Want me to send it?
```

**Why it works:**
- "Faster than a call" inverts the usual ask — you're saving them time, not asking for it
- Loom-style asynchronous video is a proven pattern with operator-audience reply rates around 15–25%
- The video is bespoke, not a generic demo recording — that's the whole point
- One-line reply unlocks it ("yes, send it") — minimum friction

---

### Email 3 — Day 11: Breakup (single sentence, no ask)

**Subject:** closing the loop
**Preview:** —

```
[FirstName] — going to stop landing in your inbox. If margin visibility ever moves up the list, my reply button still works. — Lawand
```

**Why it works:**
- One sentence. Reads in 3 seconds
- No ask, no CTA, no link, no PS — most breakups die because they hide a CTA in a PS
- Permission-to-return language ("if it ever moves up the list") keeps the door open
- Breakup emails consistently surface a 5–10% bonus reply rate because they trigger loss aversion ("wait, what was that thing?")

## 3. 15 Subject Line Templates

All lowercase. All 2–6 words. No punctuation tricks, no emojis, no first names. The job of the subject is to look like internal email a peer sent — not marketing.

### Dollar-loss (when you have a specific $ to name)

| # | Subject | Use when |
|---|---------|----------|
| 1 | `the $40K acme question` | You have a real client name + amount from public case study |
| 2 | `where margin actually goes` | First touch, no internal signal yet |
| 3 | `$1k–$20k a month` | You're pulling from the StopScopeCreep 2026 benchmark |

### Time-recovery (when the pain is hours/weeks not dollars)

| # | Subject | Use when |
|---|---------|----------|
| 4 | `39 hours / strategist / month` | First touch into ops-heavy agencies, Fluency 2026 reference |
| 5 | `56 hours of reporting` | Agency posts about reporting pain on LinkedIn |
| 6 | `the 30-day detection lag` | Email 2 in sequence, after a problem hook landed |

### Peer-proof (when you have a peer agency reference)

| # | Subject | Use when |
|---|---------|----------|
| 7 | `what [peer agency] caught` | You have permission to name a customer |
| 8 | `the agency you compete with` | You don't have permission but the peer is obvious |
| 9 | `5-person ops shift` | Generic peer-anchor, no naming required |

### Vertical-specific (when you know the agency type)

| # | Subject | Use when |
|---|---------|----------|
| 10 | `ppc margin question` | They're a paid-media agency |
| 11 | `seo retainer math` | They're a retainer-heavy SEO shop |
| 12 | `social retainer scope` | They're a social-first agency |

### Contrarian (when you want to break the pattern)

| # | Subject | Use when |
|---|---------|----------|
| 13 | `don't reply` | Email 2 or 3, breaks the "click open" reflex |
| 14 | `ignore this if growing` | Inverts the usual frame — works on confident founders |
| 15 | `wrong person?` | Sender doubts they have the right contact; flatters the reader |

**Picking rule:** Match the subject to the email body. Dollar-loss subjects need dollar amounts in the body. Don't bait-and-switch — agency owners will mark you as spam.

## 4. The Personalization Layer

Section 10.1's whole thesis: personalization is the lift. Here's the 5-minute research protocol for each prospect, and exactly three things to find before pressing send.

### What to research (5 minutes per prospect)

**1. Stack signal — what tools are they running?**
- Their job listings (Indeed, LinkedIn): job descriptions list the stack ("must know HubSpot, ClickUp, Harvest")
- Their case study pages: client report screenshots often show which PSA/PM tool generated them
- BuiltWith or Wappalyzer for the website itself
- Their team's LinkedIn profiles often name the tools they own
- **Why it matters:** if they run HubSpot + ClickUp + Harvest + Stripe, they're our exact ICP and the email writes itself

**2. Recent hire or loss signal — who joined or left in the last 90 days?**
- LinkedIn → "People" → filter by "Started in past 3 months" or "Left in past 3 months"
- Their announcements (LinkedIn posts, X, press releases)
- A new hire in ops, finance, or PM means they're feeling the gap right now. A loss means margin is under pressure.
- **Why it matters:** this is the highest-converting trigger we've seen — "you just hired a head of ops" beats every other opener

**3. Content signal — what did the founder publish in the last 30 days?**
- LinkedIn posts (especially anything about clients, hiring, growth, margin, AI)
- Podcast appearances (often listed in their LinkedIn featured section)
- Newsletter, blog, X account
- **Why it matters:** quoting the founder back to themselves is the single most disarming personalization move

### How to insert it naturally

The trap most senders fall into: "I saw on your LinkedIn that…" That phrase screams template because it announces the research. Don't do that.

**Wrong:**
> "I saw on LinkedIn that you posted about scaling your PPC team last week…"

**Right:**
> "The point you made about PPC teams stalling at 8 people — we see that exact pattern in our data."

**Wrong:**
> "Congrats on hiring Sarah Mitchell as your new Head of Ops!"

**Right:**
> "New head of ops at [agency] usually means someone's about to inherit 6 disconnected tools and a margin question nobody's answering."

The principle: speak to the *implication* of the signal, not the signal itself. The reader knows what they posted. They want to know what *you* think it means.

### The "if you can't, don't" rule

If you can't find any of the three signals in 5 minutes, don't send the email. Move on to the next prospect. The cost of a bad cold email (spam flag, brand damage, deliverability hit) is higher than the cost of skipping a prospect. We're doing 30 a day, not 300.

## 5. Six Hook Frameworks

One opening line per scenario. Each is calibrated to the agency owner's emotional state in that moment.

### Hook 1 — New CMO/Head of Ops hire at the prospect's agency

> "New head of ops at [agency] usually inherits the same first problem: pulling margin numbers out of 6 tools nobody's connected. Worth saving them a quarter of pain?"

**Why:** speaks past the founder, to the person they just hired. Founder feels seen as a helpful boss.

### Hook 2 — Recent layoff or team contraction

> "Hard call on the team reshape last month. The agencies that ran a similar cut and recovered margin all did the same thing: rebuilt visibility on per-client profitability before re-hiring. Curious if that's where you're heading."

**Why:** acknowledges the pain without rubbing it in, then offers the playbook. Layoff agencies are the most receptive — 40% of agencies had one in the past 12 months.

### Hook 3 — Client churn signal (lost logo on website, public client move)

> "Saw [client name] is no longer on the roster. 65% of agencies had clients move work in-house last year — usually after months of unflagged scope and margin slippage. We catch that pattern before it hits notice."

**Why:** churn is the most emotionally loaded signal. Naming it directly (gently) earns trust.

### Hook 4 — Tool overload (10+ tools, multiple PM systems, complex stack)

> "Counted at least 7 tools across the [agency] team's LinkedIn profiles. The agencies juggling 10+ tools is up 131% YoY. None of those tools talk to each other on the margin question — that's where we live."

**Why:** specific count + named benchmark (Basis 2026). Founder feels caught — in a useful way.

### Hook 5 — Margin compression (revenue up, profit down — visible from public signals)

> "Revenue side of [agency] looks strong from the outside — 4 wins announced this quarter. The pattern we see in agencies growing like this: per-client profit slips quietly while topline climbs. Curious if that's showing up in your reports too."

**Why:** flatters the wins, then surfaces the hidden risk. Direct path to "yes that's exactly what's happening."

### Hook 6 — Agency that's growing fast (hiring 3+, new office, funding/PE-adjacent)

> "Growth phases at [agency]'s pace usually break the same thing first: visibility. Founder still pulling client P&Ls by hand at 30+ clients is the moment a fractional COO gets hired. Cheaper option exists."

**Why:** anchors to the fractional COO comparison ($10K/month) without naming OpsRadar's price yet. Buys curiosity.

## 6. Reply Handling

Copy-paste responses for the six most common reply types. Each is calibrated to convert toward a demo without pushing.

### "Send me info"

```
[FirstName] — happy to. The fastest way to see what this would look like for [agency] specifically is a 20-min screen-share where I plug in 2–3 of your tools live. Faster than reading anything I'd send.

Thursday 2pm or Friday 11am your time?
```

**Why:** "send me info" is usually a soft brush-off. Bouncing back to a demo offer (instead of sending a deck) reframes the value as a personalized read on their agency, not a generic PDF.

---

### "We're not looking"

```
[FirstName] — fair. Most agency owners we work with said the same thing on the first touch. The one that changes their mind is usually finding a 5-figure leak on a client account they thought was healthy.

Mind if I send a 2-min note in a month with one specific thing OpsRadar would catch on [agency]?
```

**Why:** accepts the no, then asks permission for a future touch — softer than a sequence escalation. The "one specific thing" promise is concrete enough to feel safe.

---

### "Not the right time"

```
[FirstName] — got it. Most useful question: is the timing wrong because Q3 is full, or because the margin question isn't on the list yet? Different answers from us either way.
```

**Why:** "not the right time" is information-poor. The question splits it into a scheduling problem (revisit later) or a priority problem (different conversation entirely). Either answer is useful.

---

### "What's the pricing?"

```
[FirstName] — three tiers: $500, $1,500, $3,000/month. The right tier depends on integration count and whether you want the AI COO to take actions or just flag them. 20-min demo is the fastest way to know which one fits.

Want to grab a slot?
```

**Why:** answers the question directly (don't hide pricing — it signals weakness), then anchors the decision back to the demo. Names the tiers without selling them.

---

### "We already use Productive / Scoro / Asana"

```
[FirstName] — good — OpsRadar plugs into those. They're the system of record. We're the intelligence layer that sits on top and tells you what those tools never could: who's going unprofitable next month, where scope is drifting this week, what to do about it.

Worth 20 minutes to see the difference?
```

**Why:** the objection assumes overlap. The reframe positions us as additive, not competitive. Directly cribbed from the product-marketing.md objection answer.

---

### "Interested — book a call"

```
[FirstName] — perfect. Here's my calendar: [calendly link]. Pick whatever works.

Before the call, one quick ask: which 2–3 of your tools are most painful right now? I'll have OpsRadar pre-connected to a sample workspace so we can move fast.

— Lawand
```

**Why:** doesn't waste the warm reply on a back-and-forth. Drops the calendar immediately, then asks one prep question that makes the demo 10x better. Founders respect the speed.

## 8. The Sending Rhythm

**Per-domain volume:**
- Single domain (lawand@opsradar.co): 25–35 personalized emails/day max, period. Going above 40/day from a single mailbox is where deliverability starts to collapse — providers like Google and Microsoft watch the volume curve, not just the content.
- Spread sends across business hours (8am–5pm in the recipient's time zone), not in bursts.
- Reply rate above 5% buys you headroom; reply rate below 2% is a signal to slow down, not speed up.

**Warm a second domain (do this at week 6):**
- Buy a second domain variant (opsradar.io or opsradar-co.com or similar — keep the brand intact)
- Forward all email on the second domain to the primary inbox
- Use a deliverability warmup service (the category, not specific brands) for 4–6 weeks before sending real outreach
- Once warm, the second domain doubles your daily capacity to ~60/day across both — without spiking either one
- **Critical:** the secondary domain is a redundancy and capacity play, not a way to send more spam. Quality bar stays the same.

**Tools by category (not brand recommendations):**

| Tool category | What it does | When you need it |
|---------------|--------------|------------------|
| Sales engagement platform | Sequences, templates, multi-channel cadences | Today — you're doing this manually |
| Email deliverability warmup | Slowly ramps domain reputation by simulating real conversations | Before warming the second domain |
| Inbox rotation / unified sender | Sends from multiple domains, looks like one sender | When you cross 60/day total |
| Prospect data + enrichment | Find emails, verify, enrich with firmographics | Once you're past the "personal network" list |
| LinkedIn automation (use carefully) | Connection requests + DMs at controlled cadence | For the LinkedIn touches in the sequence — never above 20 connects/day |
| CRM with reply tracking | See which prospects replied, threaded by sequence | Once you have more than 50 active sequences |
| Loom-style async video | Record bespoke walkthroughs for the day-7 touch | Now — single highest-leverage upgrade |

**Two rules to never break:**

1. **Never send unsubscribes from a cold sequence.** The legal risk is real (CAN-SPAM, GDPR in the UK/EU) and the deliverability risk is bigger. Always include a one-line opt-out in the footer: "If this isn't relevant, just reply with 'no' and I'll stop."

2. **One sender, one signature, one identity.** Don't rotate fake names. Don't pretend to be an assistant booking on the founder's behalf. The whole leverage of founder-sent email evaporates the moment it stops being from the founder.
