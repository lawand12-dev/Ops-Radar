## 1. Productive.io

- **Category**: All-in-one agency management / PSA (project management + resourcing + budgets + invoicing + reporting in one data model).
- **Founded / Funding / Revenue**: Founded 2014 by Boris Vrtacnik (Zagreb, Croatia origin; now incorporated Claymont, DE). ~136 employees. Funding disclosed at ~$1.07M (REACH, Alchemist Accelerator, Scopus, Rebellion, Langenberg) — very lean, likely mostly bootstrapped. OpsRadar internal strategy note pegs ARR at $6.8M (unverifiable in public sources, treat as estimate). (Source: crunchbase.com, 2026; pitchbook.com, 2026)
- **Pricing**: Per-seat, 3-seat minimum.
  - **Essential**: $9/seat/mo (annual) / $11 (monthly)
  - **Professional**: $24/seat/mo (annual) / $28 (monthly)
  - **Ultimate**: custom
  - **Enterprise**: custom, 50+ users
  - Hidden costs: add-ons like HR time-off sync (+$2/seat/mo). Volume discounts only at 50+ users. (Source: productive.io/pricing, 2026; agencyhandy.com, 2026)
- **Target customer**: Mid-sized agencies and consultancies (15–150 people) that have outgrown spreadsheets + Asana and want a single financial source of truth. Sweet spot is the 25–75-person digital agency.
- **Killer feature**: Unified data model — time logged updates budget burn, profitability, and invoicing **with zero manual reconciliation**. Nobody else in the agency PSA space has stitched it together as cleanly. (Source: productive.io, 2026)
- **What they do well**:
  1. Best-in-class budget tracking — real-time burn vs. budget per project, per role, per service line.
  2. Strong content marketing (their blog ranks for high-intent agency keywords: "PSA software", "agency operations", "agency management software").
  3. AI features now layered in: "Productive AI" (powered by OpenAI) does report summarization, project allocation filtering, content drafting, translation. (Source: productive.io/productive-ai, 2026)
- **Where they fall short for our ICP**:
  1. **Record-keeper, not recommender** — Productive AI summarizes reports and drafts content; it does not say "Acme is 23% over scope this month, here's the unbilled work." Diagnosis lives in the dashboard; the human still has to read it.
  2. **Per-seat pricing punishes growth** — a 25-person agency on Professional is $600/mo just for licenses; that climbs linearly. OpsRadar is flat-tier, so the founder gets predictable ops spend.
  3. **No agentic actions** — there is no "send the invoice with one click from the alert", no draft-the-PM-message, no automated guardrail. It still requires the COO to convert insight into action.
  4. **Not real-time alerting** — surfaces data on dashboards you have to open; doesn't ping you in Slack/email when the metric breaks the threshold.
  5. **Implementation tax** — Productive is a system of record. Adopting it means migrating off existing PSA/PM, which is a 3–6 month change-management slog. OpsRadar bolts onto the tools the agency already runs.
- **How they position**: Hero language: "Run resources, projects, and finances in one platform." Category language: "agency management software" / "professional services automation." (Source: productive.io, 2026)
- **Their growth motion**: SEO + content-led. They dominate "best PSA software" / "best agency management software" / "[Competitor] alternatives" SERPs. Free trial → self-serve → inside sales for upsell to Ultimate/Enterprise. Light partner motion. Lean team, lean spend, content-heavy.
- **The OpsRadar opening**: Productive is the system of record; we're the intelligence layer that sits on top of it. We don't ask agencies to rip out Productive — we plug into it (and HubSpot, ClickUp, Harvest, Stripe, six more) and turn its data into actions instead of dashboards.
- **Sales line to use against them**: *"Productive will tell you Acme's margin is 14% three weeks after the month closes. OpsRadar tells you Acme is about to drop below 20% **this week** — and gives you the three unbilled deliverables to invoice for to fix it. One is a system of record. The other is a COO."*

## 2. Scoro

- **Category**: End-to-end work management / PSA for professional services. Sits squarely between Productive (agency-only) and Kantata (enterprise).
- **Founded / Funding / Revenue**: Founded 2013 in Tallinn, Estonia by Fred Krieger; HQ London with offices in Tallinn + New York. ~148 employees. Total funding ~$23.5M (Seed $1.9M 2016; Series A $5M 2018 led by Livonia; Series B $16.4M 2021 led by Kennet). Revenue: $20.8M in 2024 (up from $12M in 2023). (Source: pitchbook.com, 2026; getlatka.com, 2024; tech.eu, 2021)
- **Pricing**: Per-user, monthly, **5-seat minimum on every tier**.
  - **Essential**: $19.90/user/mo (annual) / $23.90 (monthly)
  - **Standard**: $32.90/user/mo (annual) / $38.90 (monthly)
  - **Pro**: $49.90/user/mo (annual) / $59.90 (monthly)
  - **Ultimate**: custom
  - Floor entry: ~$100/mo on Essential at 5 seats; ~$250/mo on Pro. Real-world average lands $400–$1,500/mo. (Source: scoro.com/pricing, 2026)
- **Target customer**: Mid-market services firms — agencies, consultancies, architecture, legal — 10–500 people. Slightly more "consulting" in DNA than "agency" (Productive is more agency-native).
- **Killer feature**: Real-time profitability dashboard at role, service, and project level. Strong scenario / forecasting layer.
- **What they do well**:
  1. Deepest dashboard customization in the category — executives, PMs, finance can each have a tailored live view.
  2. New "ELI Assistant" AI layer (2025–2026) lets users update projects and retrieve insights via natural language. (Source: scoro.com, 2026)
  3. Strong European brand; preferred by EU consultancies and design-heavy agencies.
- **Where they fall short for our ICP**:
  1. **Expensive for small agencies** — a 10-person agency on Pro = $499/mo just for seats; lands closer to Growth tier of OpsRadar but with no ops brain.
  2. **5-seat minimum locks out solos and 3-person teams** — the exact "I'm running this alone" persona we serve.
  3. **Consulting-flavored, not agency-shaped** — built for utilization-billing service shops; weaker on retainer-creep detection and AI-tool ROI tracking, which are agency-specific JTBDs.
  4. **AI is conversational, not autonomous** — ELI answers questions; it doesn't take action with guardrails.
  5. **Heavy onboarding** — Capterra reviews consistently flag 2–3 month implementation curves.
- **How they position**: Hero language: "Manage your entire business with one solution" / "End-to-end work management." Category: "professional services automation." Not agency-specific in copy — leans broader to capture consulting + creative + legal. (Source: scoro.com, 2026)
- **Their growth motion**: Outbound sales + content + heavy SEO on "[competitor] alternatives" pages. EU enterprise sales cycle. Trial → SDR → AE → 30-day pilot.
- **The OpsRadar opening**: Scoro is the consulting firm's PSA — built for utilization billing. OpsRadar is the agency owner's COO — built for retainer health, scope creep, and AI ROI. Different category, different price floor, different DNA.
- **Sales line to use against them**: *"Scoro starts at 5 seats and asks you to run your whole business inside it. OpsRadar starts at 1 founder and plugs into the 6 tools you already pay for. You don't need a new system — you need someone watching what the systems already know."*

## 3. Kantata

- **Category**: Enterprise PSA (project management + resourcing + financials + BI) for services-led organizations 100–10,000+ employees.
- **Founded / Funding / Revenue**: Formed February 2022 via Accel-KKR-led merger of Mavenlink (2008, US) and Kimble Applications (2011, UK). Existing investors include Goldman Sachs, Carrick Capital Partners. Serves 2,000+ clients and 500K+ services professionals. Total raised across legacy entities: $200M+ pre-merger. (Source: kantata.com, 2026; businesswire.com, 2025)
- **Pricing**: Not publicly listed. Custom quote only. Reports suggest enterprise contracts start ~$30K–$50K/year and quickly land $100K+ with implementation. Hidden costs: implementation fees, data migration, training, integration build. (Source: pricingnow.com, 2026; selecthub.com, 2026 — unverifiable list pricing, treat as estimate)
- **Target customer**: 100+ person professional services firms — global consultancies, large digital networks, IT services, big creative shops. Salesforce-native install base. Not for sub-$10M agencies.
- **Killer feature**: "Resource intelligence" — global skills graph + AI Resourcing Agent that detects overallocations and budget overruns proactively, plus AI Forecast Accelerator for revenue/margin prediction. (Source: kantata.com, 2026; channelinsider.com, 2025)
- **What they do well**:
  1. The only PSA with serious AI investment at the enterprise tier (Kantata AI launched October 2025).
  2. Deep Salesforce integration — wins Salesforce-stack RFPs by default.
  3. Mature multi-entity, multi-currency, multi-region capability.
- **Where they fall short for our ICP**:
  1. **Way too expensive and too heavy** for sub-$5M agencies — the implementation alone exceeds our annual contract value.
  2. **6+ month implementation** — the agency owner needs help this quarter, not next year.
  3. **Salesforce-stack-flavored** — agencies on HubSpot/Pipedrive face friction.
  4. **Enterprise sales cycle** — 6–9 month evaluation; small agency owner just wants to start.
  5. **AI is for resourcing, not the COO job** — no scope creep alerts, no client health scoring for a single owner-operator.
- **How they position**: Hero language: "The leading professional services automation platform." Heavy use of "industry cloud," "purpose-built for services," "operations cloud." Targets the CFO/COO/CIO of services organizations.
- **Their growth motion**: Enterprise field sales + analyst relations (Gartner Magic Quadrant). PE-backed M&A roll-up strategy. Massive AppExchange presence (Salesforce ecosystem).
- **The OpsRadar opening**: We don't compete with Kantata. We win the agencies they won't take a meeting with — sub-$5M ARR, 5–50 people, founder-led, no Salesforce, no time for a 6-month implementation.
- **Sales line to use against them**: *"Kantata is what you buy when you're a 300-person agency network and your CFO needs a dashboard for the board. You're 12 people. You don't need PSA software — you need someone watching your margin while you're in client meetings."*

## 4. Accelo

- **Category**: Client work management / PSA for mid-market service businesses (PSA + CRM + retainer automation in one).
- **Founded / Funding / Revenue**: Founded 2011 in Wollongong, Australia; HQ now San Francisco Bay Area with Denver office. Acquired by Bow River Capital 2024. Revenue: $15M (2023) → $21.4M (recent estimate) → $35M (June 2025). 1,500+ customers (2023). 2024 enterprise sales bookings +83.5% YoY. (Source: getlatka.com, 2023; tracxn.com, 2026; konaequity.com, 2025)
- **Pricing**: Quote-on-request (no public list). Estimated:
  - **Core**: ~$20/user/mo
  - **Professional**: ~$50/user/mo
  - **Advanced**: ~$90/user/mo
  - Typically 5–10 seat minimum; real entry $250–$900/mo. (Source: getpulsesignal.com, 2026; itqlick.com, 2026 — unverifiable, treat as estimate)
- **Target customer**: Mid-market service businesses with recurring retainer revenue — agencies, IT MSPs, consulting firms, accounting firms — 10–100 people. More multi-vertical than Productive.
- **Killer feature**: Retainer + recurring billing automation — handles service-level agreements, recurring invoicing, and time-against-retainer better than most PSAs.
- **What they do well**:
  1. Strong retainer/ticketing model for agencies that bill ongoing work.
  2. Embedded CRM = single tool from lead → quote → project → invoice (less stack sprawl).
  3. Workflow automation engine for repetitive client-facing processes.
- **Where they fall short for our ICP**:
  1. **No AI intelligence layer** — built around automation of known processes, not detection of unknown problems.
  2. **CRM-included is friction, not feature** — most of our ICP already uses HubSpot or Pipedrive and won't switch.
  3. **Opaque pricing creates demo-call friction** — agencies want to self-serve.
  4. **No real-time owner alerts** — surfaces problems in dashboards/reports, not in the founder's inbox/Slack the moment they emerge.
  5. **Cross-vertical positioning dilutes agency fit** — benchmarks aren't agency-specific.
- **How they position**: Hero language: "PSA software to power your operations." Category: "client work management." (Source: accelo.com, 2026)
- **Their growth motion**: Inbound demos + SDR-led outbound. Strong G2 / Capterra reviews program. Bow River Capital backing is fueling mid-market AE expansion.
- **The OpsRadar opening**: Accelo wants to be your whole stack. We want to be the brain on top of the stack you already have. Different bet — Accelo bets agencies will consolidate; we bet they won't, and they need intelligence across the tools they actually use.
- **Sales line to use against them**: *"Accelo automates the work you already know how to do. OpsRadar catches the work you didn't know was bleeding you — the overserviced client, the scope drift, the AI subscription nobody's using. Automation is table stakes now. Intelligence is the moat."*

## 5. Float

- **Category**: Resource management / capacity planning. Best-of-breed point tool, not a PSA.
- **Founded / Funding / Revenue**: Founded 2011 (originally Australia, now 100% remote). ~50 employees. Revenue: $15M (2024). 4,500+ professional services teams; 3,500+ paying customers. B Corp certified March 2024. (Source: getlatka.com, 2024; float.com, 2026)
- **Pricing**: Per-person scheduled:
  - **Starter**: $7/person/mo
  - **Pro**: $12/person/mo (adds actuals, estimates vs actuals, SSO)
  - **Enterprise**: custom (CSM, SLAs, unlimited placeholders)
  - Time tracking add-on. (Source: float.com/pricing, 2026)
- **Target customer**: Mid-market agencies and consultancies, 10–200 people. Particularly loved by design studios, dev shops, and creative agencies that bill by the hour.
- **Killer feature**: "Talent Graph™" — a living map of people, skills, work, and results that powers staffing recommendations. Plus MCP integration with ChatGPT/Claude (you can ask Float about utilization in natural language inside your AI tool). (Source: float.com/ai, 2026)
- **What they do well**:
  1. The cleanest UX in resource scheduling — drag-and-drop calendar that ops managers actually enjoy using.
  2. Fast, focused product — does one thing brilliantly.
  3. Solid forecasting + utilization reports + capacity-vs-demand views.
- **Where they fall short for our ICP**:
  1. **One slice only** — capacity. Zero visibility into client profitability, scope creep, AI ROI, cash flow, or invoice health.
  2. **Doesn't recommend; it schedules** — Float shows you who's overallocated; it doesn't say "fire this client" or "raise this rate."
  3. **No financial layer** — margin per client is invisible without bolting on a PSA.
  4. **Per-person pricing scales with team, not with value** — 30-person agency on Pro = $360/mo for capacity alone.
  5. **Best paired with, not replaced by** — Float users still need 4 other tools to run the agency.
- **How they position**: Hero language: "The #1 solution for profitable resource management." Category: "resource management software" / "capacity planning." Targets ops managers, not founders. (Source: float.com, 2026)
- **Their growth motion**: PLG-first — generous trial, self-serve signup, in-product upgrade. Strong SEO on "capacity planning" and "resource management" SERPs. Light sales motion above 50 seats.
- **The OpsRadar opening**: Float owns the capacity slice. We own the entire operations brain — and we integrate with Float (and Harvest, ClickUp, etc.) to pull capacity data into the broader margin/scope/cash picture. They sell to the ops manager. We sell to the owner.
- **Sales line to use against them**: *"Float tells you your team is 92% booked. Great. It doesn't tell you that 40% of that capacity is going to a client losing you money. Capacity without margin is just busy."*

## 6. Resource Guru

- **Founded / Funding / Revenue**: Founded May 2012 in London by Andrew Rogoff and Percy Stilwell. ~22 employees. Revenue: $6.1M (2024). 60,000+ users across 88 countries. Customers include NASA, Accenture, KPMG, Ogilvy, PwC. Total funding ~$1.14M (Seed 2014, Index Ventures). Effectively bootstrapped. (Source: getlatka.com, 2024; growthbusiness.co.uk; resourceguruapp.com, 2026)
- **Pricing**: Per-person/mo (annual):
  - **Grasshopper**: $4.16
  - **Blackbelt**: $6.65
  - **Master**: $10
  - Monthly: $5 / $8 / $12. Placeholders charged at per-person rate. Other resources (rooms/equipment) priced separately. (Source: resourceguruapp.com/pricing, 2026)
- **Target customer**: SMB teams (5–50 people) needing simple scheduling — agencies, design studios, dev shops, internal IT teams. More entry-level than Float.
- **Killer feature**: Conflict-free scheduling with clash management + the cleanest single-screen team calendar in the category.
- **What they do well**:
  1. Lowest price point in resource scheduling — small agency budget-friendly.
  2. Strong API + Zapier (1,500+ app connections) + new Zapier MCP support.
  3. 30-day free trial, no card needed — frictionless self-serve.
- **Where they fall short for our ICP**:
  1. **Scheduling only** — no margin, no project profitability, no AI, no recommendations.
  2. **No native AI** — relies on third-party MCP/Zapier to bolt AI on.
  3. **No alerts engine** — passive tool, you go to it; it doesn't come to you.
  4. **Bare-bones forecasting** — capacity heatmaps, not predictive analytics.
  5. **No financial integration** — doesn't connect to Stripe / Xero / Harvest natively to tie hours to dollars.
- **How they position**: Hero language: "The world's most-loved resource scheduling software." Category: "resource scheduling." Stays narrow on purpose. (Source: resourceguruapp.com, 2026)
- **Their growth motion**: Bootstrapped PLG — content (resource management guides), word of mouth, freemium-style trial. Famous logos used as social proof. Tiny team, tiny CAC.
- **The OpsRadar opening**: Resource Guru is the cheapest, friendliest scheduler. We aren't a scheduler — we sit above it and turn its data into margin alerts the owner actually needs. If they love Resource Guru, great — keep it, plug it in.
- **Sales line to use against them**: *"You're paying $200/mo for a calendar that tells you who's busy. OpsRadar is $500/mo for a system that tells you whether being busy is making you any money."*

Bootstrapped for a decade; one undisclosed funding round (not Insight Partners — that's unverified). (Source: agencyanalytics.com/about; pitchbook.com, 2026)

  - **Freelancer**: $59/mo (5 clients, 5 users)
  - **Agency**: $179/mo (10 clients, unlimited users)
  - **Agency Pro**: $349/mo (15 clients, adds AI insights, anomaly detection, forecasting, API)
  - **Enterprise**: custom (25+ clients)
  - Add-ons: +$20/mo per extra client; Rank Tracker +$50/mo. (Source: agencyanalytics.com/pricing, 2026)
- **Target customer**: Marketing agencies (SEO, PPC, social) that need to send beautiful, branded reports to clients showing campaign performance. 1–50 person agencies. The "client management theater" tool.
- **Killer feature**: 80+ marketing integrations (GA, GAds, Meta, LinkedIn, GSC, Ahrefs, SEMrush, Moz, etc.) → automated white-label client reports. Now with MCP for Claude/ChatGPT, AI Insights, and Anomaly Detection (Agency Pro tier and up). (Source: agencyanalytics.com/features/insights, 2026)
- **What they do well**:
  1. Best-in-class for marketing-data report automation.
  2. White-label dashboards clients log into = retention tool for the agency.
  3. Anomaly Detection on client *marketing* metrics (CPC spike, ranking drop) is genuinely useful.
- **Where they fall short for our ICP**:
  1. **Wrong audience entirely** — it's client-facing, not owner-facing. Surfaces marketing performance, not agency profitability.
  2. **Zero internal ops layer** — no utilization, no margin per client, no scope creep, no capacity, no cash flow.
  3. **Anomaly detection is client-data, not agency-data** — flags that Acme's CTR dropped, not that Acme is unprofitable.
  4. **No PSA integrations** — doesn't pull from Harvest, ClickUp, Stripe.
  5. **AI features are gated behind $349 Pro tier** — most agencies buy down-tier.
- **How they position**: Hero language: "All-in-one marketing reporting platform for agencies." Category: "marketing reporting" / "client reporting." (Source: agencyanalytics.com, 2026)
- **Their growth motion**: PLG via 14-day free trial + 30-day money-back guarantee. Massive SEO footprint on "marketing reporting" terms. Strong agency-community marketing (sponsorships, partnerships).
- **The OpsRadar opening**: AgencyAnalytics shows your clients how their *campaigns* are doing. OpsRadar shows you, the owner, how your *agency* is doing. Different problem, different person, both belong in the stack.
- **Sales line to use against them**: *"AgencyAnalytics is your client-facing reporting. OpsRadar is your internal reality. One keeps the client; the other keeps the bank account."*

## 8. ClickUp

- **Category**: Horizontal work management platform with AI agents. The "we already use this" objection.
- **Founded / Funding / Revenue**: Founded 2016 by Zeb Evans and Alex Yurkowski. 100,000+ paying customers, 10M+ users worldwide. Revenue: $300M (2025), up from $278.5M (2024). $537.5M total raised across 4 rounds (Series C $400M 2021, Series A $35M 2020). $4B valuation (2021). Likely IPO 2026–2027. Acquired Qatalog (100+ integrations + ActionQuery agent engine) in 2025. (Source: getlatka.com, 2025; sacra.com, 2026; siliconangle.com, 2026)
- **Pricing**: Per-user/mo (annual):
  - **Free Forever**: $0 (100 automations/mo limit)
  - **Unlimited**: $7/user/mo (annual) / $10 (monthly)
  - **Business**: $12/user/mo (annual) / $19 (monthly)
  - **Enterprise**: custom
  - **AI add-on (Brain)**: +$9/user/mo (or +$28 for Super Agents tier) — doubles your bill if AI is essential. (Source: clickup.com/pricing, 2026; aiproductivity.ai, 2026)
- **Target customer**: Anyone with a team. ClickUp's strength and weakness — it's horizontal. Agencies are a meaningful segment but they share the product with engineering teams, schools, marketing departments, etc.
- **Killer feature**: "Super Agents" (2026) — persistent-memory AI agents triggered by schedule/automation/@mention that own work end-to-end (e.g., a Campaign Status Agent that pulls task completion every Friday and ships a client-ready summary). (Source: siliconangle.com, 2026; clickup.com/brain, 2026)
- **What they do well**:
  1. Insane breadth — list/board/calendar/Gantt/whiteboard/docs/chat/forms all in one.
  2. Aggressive AI roadmap — Brain + Super Agents are credible.
  3. Massive ecosystem and 100+ integrations via Qatalog acquisition.
  4. Generous free tier = land-everywhere growth.
- **Where they fall short for our ICP**:
  1. **Horizontal, not agency-shaped** — no agency benchmarks, no PSA financials, no retainer margin, no utilization-by-team-size norms.
  2. **AI add-on doubles the bill** — agency owners get sticker shock at the Business+Brain combo.
  3. **Configuration tax** — to make ClickUp useful for agency ops takes weeks of setup. That's why ZenPilot exists as a $10K–$50K business.
  4. **Super Agents are workflow agents, not COO agents** — they execute defined tasks, not "watch the whole agency and tell me what's wrong."
  5. **No financial integration to Stripe / Xero / QuickBooks at the agency-margin layer** — your task system isn't your P&L.
- **How they position**: Hero language: "One app to replace them all." Category: "everything app" / "work AI" / "AI productivity platform." (Source: clickup.com, 2026)
- **Their growth motion**: PLG + freemium + extreme paid acquisition (one of the biggest SaaS ad spenders) + content + agency partner network (ZenPilot is the marquee one).
- **The OpsRadar opening**: ClickUp is where the work happens. OpsRadar is the COO that watches the work happen across ClickUp, Harvest, Stripe, and 7 more tools — and tells the founder what's going wrong. We integrate with ClickUp; we don't compete with it.
- **Sales line to use against them**: *"You already use ClickUp. Good. Now who's actually watching it? Who tells you that the Acme retainer is 30% over budget this month and which deliverables to invoice for? That's not a ClickUp problem — that's the COO problem ClickUp will never solve for you. We're the COO that lives on top of ClickUp."*
