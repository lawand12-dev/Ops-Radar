## Step 2 — Discovery

**Section H2:**
> **Step 02 · Discovery. We learn how your agency actually runs.**

**Body (preserved voice):**
> An AI COO is not a template. It's a system built around the specific operations of your agency — and the only way to build it correctly is to understand your agency first. Discovery is the gate. We do not build an AI COO for an agency we do not understand.

**What happens:**
- You fill out a structured questionnaire on tools, clients, team, and the workflows you repeat every week.
- We run one or two working sessions to map the things only insiders know — the unspoken rules, the recurring fires, the clients you over-service without realizing.

**What you get:**
A written read-out of your current-state ops — where the leaks are, what an AI COO would watch first, and whether the Blueprint is worth your money. If we don't see at least 5× the Blueprint price in recoverable leak, we say so before you pay for the Blueprint.

## What's NOT in the path (objection-handler)

**Section H2:**
> **What you might be expecting — and what's actually here.**

### "Why isn't there a free trial?"
A free trial means handing you a half-configured AI COO and hoping you connect enough tools to see value before the trial ends. Most people don't. So instead: a free 20-minute call, a free 3-minute audit, and a 30-day money-back guarantee once you're paying. If your AI COO isn't earning its keep in month one, we send the money back the same day. Trial-equivalent risk, real-product result.

### "Can I skip Discovery and go straight to Build?"
No. Every Build we've shipped that skipped Discovery has shipped the wrong system. We can't price the Build without it, and we can't tune the AI COO to your agency without it. Discovery is $500 — less than the first leak it usually surfaces.

### "Can I onboard in less than a week?"
For Discovery, no — we need a week to read, reason, and write. For Build, sometimes yes: Starter-tier agencies with 3 integrations can be live in 7–10 days from Blueprint sign-off. Pro tier with agentic actions enabled takes the full 2–3 weeks because the safety net needs trust time.

### "What if I get the Blueprint and decide not to Build?"
The Blueprint is yours. Use it to hire someone else, DIY it, or shelve it. No follow-up sequence, no obligation. About 1 in 6 Blueprints don't go to Build — usually because the owner realizes they need to fix something operationally before software can help. That's a fine outcome.

### "What do I actually own after Build?"
Everything. Workflows run on your accounts and your credentials. OBSERVE reads your stack live — nothing is cached in our database. If OpsRadar disappeared tomorrow, your AI COO keeps running. You or anyone you hire can maintain it.

## Risk flags

1. **Discovery/Blueprint prices need user confirmation.** `pricing-depth.md` is exhaustive on the three subscription tiers ($500/$1,500/$3,000) and the Fractional Operator SKU ($2,500 + $250/agency), but does NOT name a Discovery price or a Blueprint price. The brief from the user states "$500/$1500/$3000 + $2500/Fractional Operator" — those are the *subscription* tiers, not Discovery/Blueprint. I have proposed **$500 for Discovery** and **$1,500 for Blueprint** (total $2,000, credited back to month one of subscription if Build proceeds), reasoning: (a) it matches the lowest subscription tier as the floor for Discovery so it doesn't feel weightier than the product, (b) Blueprint at $1,500 anchors to the Growth tier so the buyer sees price continuity, (c) $2,000 total upfront is below the $5K fractional COO floor and small enough that an owner can decide without a board. **User must confirm these numbers before this copy ships.**

2. **The audit-demotion call is a strategic decision.** I chose to **promote** the audit (move it from demoted italic note into a dedicated panel that frames it as a parallel entry point). The risk of the alternative — removing all audit mentions on /how-it-works — is that the highest-traffic asset on the site (the 3-minute audit) loses one of its biggest internal-link slots and the page becomes call-only. Promoting it preserves the lower-friction path for cold-email leads who aren't ready to book a calendar. If the user disagrees, the fallback is "remove the audit mention entirely from this page and rely on nav-level linking." Flagging both.

3. **"Live in ~30 days" is a specific delivery time** — `product-marketing.md` banned-words list explicitly forbids "specific delivery times ('48-hour audit')." The 30-day figure is presented as "typical" / "typically 25–35 days" / "live in ~30 days" with hedge language to stay in safe-harbor territory, but the user should confirm this is acceptable. If not, the fallback is "live in weeks, not months" — softer, still answers the buyer's question, no specific number.

4. **"First dollar typically recovered in week 4"** in the Build pricing table is the most aggressive claim on the page. It's defensible from `pricing-depth.md` §1.1 (Starter tier value math shows ~$4,300/mo of recoverable value at $500 price), but it's a single-customer claim made on a page that doesn't yet have testimonials. Either (a) keep with the hedge "typically," (b) soften to "usually pays for itself in the first month," or (c) remove the line until customer #5 confirms it on the record. Recommend (b) as the safest ship-now choice.
