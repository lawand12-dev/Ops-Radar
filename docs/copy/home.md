### Why this hero, copywriter notes

**The H1 does three jobs in 8 words.**
- *Hire* = it's software (verb tells you product type — you hire on a SaaS, not on a service).
- *AI workforce* = the category (no ambiguity about LLM agents).
- *Keep the brain it builds* = the moat-as-promise (data sovereignty + accumulated value + ownership all in five words).

**The subhead does three more jobs in 29 words.**
- "OpsRadar gives every agency" = who it's for (wedge).
- "28 named AI employees that run your operations across [tools]" = what it does + integration breadth.
- "writes everything they learn into a Notion you own forever" = the brain-as-asset hook, without the EBITDA math (moved to Section 1 where it has space to land).

**The sub-subhead is the hook line.**
"Riley watches your revenue. Cole drafts collections." — named characters in service of a concrete outcome. Reader curiosity now wants to know who the other 24 are. They scroll. The page wins the scroll.

**What we explicitly kept from v4:**
- Brain-as-asset positioning (moved from H1 to Section 1, where it has more room than a hero line can hold)
- Earned autonomy framing (still in Section 4, untouched)
- Fractional Operator callout (Section 5, untouched)

**What we explicitly dropped from v4:**
- "The agency you can sell" H1 — too narrow (only resonates with owners thinking about exit) and didn't tell the visitor what the product IS
- EBITDA math in the hero — too niche for the H1 surface (80% of agency owners don't think in multiples), kept in Section 1 where readers who care will see it

## Section 4 — The control surface (earned autonomy, the anti-rogue-agent story)

**Section eyebrow:** `WHY OPSRADAR IS NOT 11X`

**Section H2:** `Earned autonomy. Not blind autonomy.`

**Section body (66 words):**
`The horror stories you've read about AI agents — sending wrong emails, billing wrong customers, deleting wrong data — share one architecture: autonomous from day one, no audit, no graduation. OpsRadar starts every high-risk action in your Actions queue. You approve, reject, or edit. After you've approved a routine action 30 times at 90%+ rate, OpsRadar promotes it to auto-fire with notification. The agents earn autonomy. They don't get it for free.`

**Receipt bullets:**
- `Risk-tiered defaults: high-risk actions (firing a client, mass email) ALWAYS ask. Medium-risk actions (drafting replies, scheduling meetings) graduate to Auto after earning trust. Low-risk actions (Notion writes, internal logs) fire silently.`
- `Per-tool autonomy override: Ask, Auto, Off — you set the floor per tool, OpsRadar earns the ceiling.`
- `Approval queue at /dashboard/actions — see everything pending in one place, with the confidence score the agent assigned.`
- `Every action logged with the agent that proposed it, the data it saw, and the reason it gave.`
- `Reject something and OpsRadar learns the rule for next time (see: Apprentice Mode above).`

| AI Operating System, AI workforce, AI employee, company brain, your Notion | AI COO (as the category label — fine inside as a job title only), assistant, copilot, platform, dashboard (generic) |
