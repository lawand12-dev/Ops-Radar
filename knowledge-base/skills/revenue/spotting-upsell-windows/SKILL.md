# Spotting Upsell Windows

*"Should I pitch Acme on more work? Or is now the wrong moment?"*

Most agency owners under-upsell their best accounts and over-upsell the wrong ones. The miss isn't the offer — it's the timing. Your job: name the signal, confirm the moment, and hand the owner the words.

## When this triggers

A client is in an upsell window when 1+ of these are true AND timing is clean (see next section):

1. **They just hit a measurable win.** A campaign performed, a launch shipped, a number moved in their direction. The owner can name it.
2. **They're hitting the retainer cap repeatedly.** Two or more months of logged hours pressing against budget — not creeping over (that's scope creep), but consistently filling it. Demand is real.
3. **They're asking about adjacent services.** "Do you also do X?" or "Have you ever helped with Y?" appears more than once in recent comms. They're already shopping.
4. **Engagement is deepening.** More stakeholders on calls, faster reply times, the founder is now in the thread instead of the marketing manager. They're bringing you closer.
5. **A renewal or anniversary is within 60 days.** Natural conversation moment is already on the calendar.

One signal alone isn't enough. Two stacked is the window.

## What to do

Before recommending the conversation, run the **timing check**. The signal can be loud and the moment can still be wrong.

| Timing check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Has the win actually landed? | Right after a win = green. Pre-win or during execution = red. |
| Any unresolved issue in the last 30 days? | Open complaint, missed deadline, billing dispute = red. Pitch later. |
| Has scope been the topic recently? | If you just had the 1st/2nd/3rd-ask conversation, do not stack an upsell on top. |
| Is the right person in the room? | Upsell to the budget owner, not the operator. If the founder hasn't been on a call recently, wait for one. |
| Does `__how_we_work` allow it? | Some customers' rules say "no upsells mid-contract" — respect that. |

If all five are green, surface to the owner with: the named signal, the win or moment it's tied to, the specific expansion you'd propose, and the script below.

The framing the owner gives the client should always be **anchored to the win**, not to the agency's revenue need. "You just saw X happen — here's how we'd compound it" lands. "We'd like to grow the account" doesn't.

## Conversation script

The offer:
> "We've been thinking about [client name] since [the win — name it specifically]. The piece we haven't built out yet is [the adjacent service / next tier]. If we layered that in, the realistic next 90 days would look like [outcome — keep it concrete]. We'd structure it as [retainer expansion / project add-on / new tier] at [price]. Want us to put a one-pager in front of you, or is now not the right moment?"

Two things matter in that script:

- **Tied to a specific win they recognize.** Not "you've been a great client."
- **An explicit out at the end.** "Not the right moment" is a real answer. Giving the option makes the "yes" feel less like a sales push.

The "no" recovery:
> "Totally fair — appreciate you telling me. Mind if I check back at [renewal date / quarter-end / after Y ships]? And if anything shifts on your end before then — budget, priorities, a new service you're shopping for — just say the word."

The recovery does three jobs: protects the relationship, schedules the next attempt to a real moment (not a guess), and invites the client to surface the next signal themselves.

## What NOT to do

- Don't pitch in the same conversation as a scope creep or pricing complaint. Stacking compounds the friction.
- Don't lead with "we'd like to grow the account." Lead with the client's win.
- Don't propose an upsell without a specific named scope — vague pitches read as fishing.
- Don't pitch the operator if the budget owner exists. Wait for them to be in the room.
- Don't surface the recommendation to anyone except the agency owner — never directly to the client team.
- Don't re-pitch a "no" inside the same 30 days. Use the recovery script's anchor date.
