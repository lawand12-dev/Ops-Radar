## Why most automation backlogs stall

Teams usually start with a list of **ideas**, not a map of **failure points**. That creates a pattern where a new tool is added, a few zaps appear, and the messy handoff between sales and delivery still breaks every week.

The better approach is to audit the system in layers:

1. Identify the trigger that starts the workflow.
2. Measure every decision point that changes routing or priority.
3. Track the handoff where context moves from one team to another.

If one of those layers is ambiguous, automation only amplifies the ambiguity.

> Automate the constraint, not the noise around it.

## Run the audit in three passes

### 1. Trigger quality

Start by asking what actually causes the workflow to begin. For most sales ops teams, the trigger is one of these:

- A form submission
- A CRM stage change
- A booking confirmation
- A payment event

The trigger should answer two questions immediately:

- Is the lead or customer real?
- Is the record complete enough to route without human cleanup?

If the answer is no, the automation backlog is downstream of a data quality problem.

### 2. Decision logic

Next, inspect the rules that decide who owns the record and what happens next. This is where AI can help, but only if the operating rules are explicit.

| Layer | Question to ask | Good signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routing | Does ownership change based on deal type or geography? | Rules are written and versioned |
| Prioritization | What makes one lead urgent? | Score thresholds exist |
| Qualification | What information blocks progression? | Required fields are enforced |

If your routing logic lives inside Slack messages and rep intuition, fix that before you introduce an LLM.

### 3. Handoff latency

Most revenue systems break during handoff, not capture. Measure the time between:

- Lead qualified -> account executive notified
- Deal closed -> implementation team briefed
- Onboarding sold -> kickoff assets created

If those gaps are measured in hours or days, you have an operating system problem, not a dashboard problem.

## A practical scoring model

Use a simple scorecard before choosing any automation platform:

```yaml
scorecard:
  trigger_quality: 4
  routing_logic: 2
  handoff_latency_hours: 17
  required_manual_touches: 6
  system_owner: revenue_ops
```

This is intentionally simple. You are trying to decide **where the system leaks**, not produce a perfect maturity model.

## Where AI actually helps

Once the workflow is stable, AI becomes useful in a few specific jobs:

- Summarizing sales calls into CRM-ready notes
- Classifying inbound leads against an ICP rubric
- Drafting implementation briefs from closed-won records
- Flagging missing data before a deal can move stages

These are high-leverage because they remove interpretation work, not because they sound advanced.

## What to automate first

If you want one default order, use this:

1. CRM data cleanup and field enforcement
2. Lead routing and assignment
3. Sales-to-delivery handoff packaging
4. Reporting and weekly summaries
5. AI enrichment and prioritization

That sequence gives you cleaner data, faster response times, and a better surface area for AI to work on later.
